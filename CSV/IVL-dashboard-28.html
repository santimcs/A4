Method & rationale — how to read this (collapsible)

Click to expand the background notes

Why this section exists. The initial question was “how often does a buy-after-a-sharp-drop → sell-into-a-sharp-rise pattern occur, and can I concentrate when it does?” The original closed-trade list is not a base rate — it only shows trades that were actually taken. This table scans the entire price history so we can see the denominator: how many qualifying setups existed, and how many would have paid off.

Why we switched from annualized to realized %. The earlier version ranked events by annualized return, (1+r)^(365/days) − 1, which is exponential in 1/days and dominates the ranking with 2–4 day holds regardless of gain size. The table now shows realized % — the simple gain from entry to the first close at or above the +5% target — which is what you would actually book on a real fill. Annualized numbers are still recoverable from cal_days and realized_pct in the JSON sidecar if needed.

Why the 200-MA gate matters. In the sample, down-days that occurred below the 200-MA have a much lower ≤20-row hit rate than those above it — they are often the first leg of a real decline (e.g. Apr 2024, Nov–Dec 2024). The tile “200-MA gate: 75% vs 75%” is the single most useful refinement. If you want a mechanical alert, require price > 200-MA at the down-day close before flagging.

Why we do not recommend “concentrate”. A ≥3% down-day followed by a +5% target within 5–10 rows is a real but small-edge trade: hit rates in this sample are 44%–75%, the wins are capped near +5%, and the misses (especially below the MA) can give back more than that. The expected value is positive but thin; position size should reflect that, not the annualized number. Historical base rate: 58% hit within 10 rows, median 4 rows, worst miss -22.96%.

Data caveats in the source CSV. (1) The exported 3-yrs-sales-data.csv contains a handful of exact-duplicate rows (e.g. 2024-12-12, 2025-01-28, 2026-10-08); they are harmless here but would double-count a down-day if the duplicate itself qualified — dedupe on date before trusting absolute counts. (2) Thai holiday gaps compress calendar-day counts; the table reports both Rows (trading) and Cal days so you can choose. (3) The 200-MA is rolling(200) with min_periods=200, so the first 199 rows are excluded from the MA gate — that is why early down-days show below even when the trend looked up.

What to fine-tune next. Change DROP_PCT, TARGET_PCT, HORIZONS_ROWS, or MA_GATE_WINDOW at the top of this cell; the HTML and the JSON sidecar both regenerate deterministically. Suggested experiments: (a) tighten to DROP_PCT=0.05 — base rate drops but win size rises; (b) loosen TARGET_PCT=0.02 — hit rate jumps toward 80–90%; (c) set MA_GATE_WINDOW=50 to see whether a faster trend filter catches the Apr-2024 failure earlier. Log each variant's JSON to compare.
